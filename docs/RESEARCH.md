# Research: route media and bulk work, not small edits

OpenRouter's public traffic is carried by cheap open-weight "flash" models doing coding-agent work. DeepSeek alone carried 33.7% of 802.84T tokens over 30 days, and coding apps used 83% of the tokens of the top 100 apps, with Claude Code the #2 app (Source: OpenRouter (openrouter.ai/rankings), as of 2026-11-09T04:00:01.004Z. Licensed under CC BY 4.0; Source: OpenRouter (openrouter.ai/apps), as of 2026-11-09T04:00:18.014Z. Licensed under CC BY 4.0). Those models run a 10–49-turn Claude Code session for $0.024–0.038, against $0.534 on Claude Sonnet 5.5 and $1.331 on Claude Opus 5.5 (Source: OpenRouter Data API (openrouter.ai/docs/cookbook/administration/data-api), as of 2026-11-02T07:25:14.332Z. Licensed under CC BY 4.0). The clean benchmarks complicate that price gap. On chat and coding preference, the cheap models sit within the confidence interval of Sonnet 5.5, but on LMArena's agent board Opus 5.5 scores 0.201 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 0.024. Delegation also has a measured fixed cost. Each hand-off costs about $0.04–0.12 of Sonnet usage before the worker spends anything, so small coding tasks cost *more* Claude usage when delegated, even to a free model. For the Predictive Model Engine (PME), that points to three routes. It should always route the work Claude cannot do (audio, video, image/audio/video generation, embeddings, more than 1M tokens of context). It should route bulk one-shot text jobs and cross-family reviews by default. And it should treat agentic coding delegation as a quality-for-cost trade that only pays above roughly ten turns of lead-model work.

Stakes sharpen those routes. Of the **104 distinct use cases** in the catalog, 84 are stakes 1 or 2 at their first-listed level, where a cheap model with light review is the right default. Seven are stakes 4 (security-critical code, production database migrations, infrastructure and firewall changes, PII redaction, two-family reviews of high-risk diffs, replies sent to people, and money decisions). Those stay on Claude or on a local tool, except the two-family review, whose output only feeds Claude's decision. The strongest delegation wins at stakes 2 and 3 come where a mechanical check verifies a cheap model's output for almost nothing: tests and type checks for bulk migrations, build-and-coverage filters for generated tests, placeholder parity for translations, schema and arithmetic for invoices. Applying stakes changed the routing of 13 of the first 87 use cases. The 104 are 87 that survived honest merging of usage-grounded and gap-grounded lists, plus 17 found by a stakes review. Each has a recommended model, alternatives with Claude on equal footing, stakes, cost per run, path and verification level in the catalog; an index is in the last section, and the model facts and scores are kept with the engine.

## Coding agents on flash models carry most OpenRouter tokens

### DeepSeek, GLM and GPT-6 Luna dominate; Claude is the largest closed family

OpenRouter carried **802.84T public tokens in the 30 days to 2026-11-08**. Daily volume ran from 18.34T to 33.59T. Two DeepSeek V4.1 Flash snapshots led with 17.61% and 11.71%, followed by GLM-5.3-Flash (7.11%), Step 5 Preview (4.25%), GPT-6 Luna (4.11%), Tencent Hy4 Preview (3.88%) and **Claude Opus 5.5 at 3.66%, the seventh model and the largest Claude** (RD, as of 2026-11-09T04:00:01.004Z). By author, DeepSeek held 33.7% over 30 days and 38.5% over the last 7. Z-ai held 10.0%, OpenAI 8.2%, Tencent 7.0% and **Anthropic 6.9%**. Models with a Hugging Face id, a proxy for open weights, carried **70.3%** of tokens, and models without one carried 20.8% (RD joined with the [OpenRouter model list](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models), fetched 2026-11-08). New releases take traffic fast. The 2026-10-17 DeepSeek snapshot reached **26.75% of the last week's tokens** within three weeks of release, and Hy4 entered the top 50 on 2026-11-04 and took 6.11% of that week (RD, as of 2026-11-09T04:00:01.004Z). Usage priors therefore go stale within days. The registry also has to merge dated snapshots by family, or one model's evidence and traffic will look split. Two caveats apply to all of these numbers. They measure volume, not success. And zero-data-retention and private traffic are excluded at the source ([Data API docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/cookbook/administration/data-api)), which is likely the traffic most like p0ssess's privacy-default users.

### Claude Code is the second-largest app and the fastest-growing

Among the top 100 apps, which together used 256.72T tokens, the **coding category held 212.90T (83%)**, and the `cli-agent` subcategory alone held 200.90T. Hermes Agent led with 52.73T tokens and 481.7M requests. **Claude Code was second with 45.87T tokens and 416.0M requests, and first on the trending list**, which ranks growth over the prior three periods. Kilo Code (23.18T), pi (17.60T), OpenClaw (13.47T) and Codex (12.24T) followed (AR, as of 2026-11-09T04:00:18.014Z). Two call shapes stand out. Agent harnesses send about 100–260K tokens per request: Claude Code averages 110.3K, Cursor 118.1K and Zed 193.6K. Service-style apps send small calls: Context7 averages 2.9K tokens per request and Portkey 12.4K (AR, same as_of). These two shapes match the PME's two paths, the long sandboxed agent session and the single direct call. The scale of Claude Code on OpenRouter is itself the best demand signal p0ssess has: a lot of Claude Code work already runs on non-Anthropic models. Media apps (Descript 1.23T, Digen AI, Mira) are a small token slice, but that is mostly a measurement artifact. Image, transcription and embedding endpoints bill in units other than tokens and drop out of these rankings ([Data API docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/cookbook/administration/data-api)).

### Requests are mostly classification; tokens are mostly agents and code

OpenRouter tags a sample of traffic by task. **By requests, General work leads at 59.8%, driven by classification and tagging at 35.0%** of all classified requests, followed by data extraction at 11.6%. **By tokens, the split is nearly even: Agent 32.4%, General 30.8%, Code 30.3%, Data 6.5%.** The largest single token pool is agent workflow execution at **21.8%**, then classification (10.0%), code generation (9.9%), multi-step planning (7.8%) and debugging (6.2%) (CT, as of 2026-11-08). Within most tags the same few models lead, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash and GPT-6 Luna, so popularity barely separates the use cases. The exceptions are informative. Step 5 Preview carries **83% of summarization tokens**. GPT-6 Luna carries 26% of customer-support requests. Classifier models such as Typesafe's jev-1.13, which has no clean benchmark evidence at all, take 23% of classification requests (CT, as of 2026-11-08). Context size follows the agent workload. The 1M bucket held **126.67T of the week's 207.49T tokens (61%)**, while the 1K and 10K buckets are dominated by purpose-built classifiers (RD, context filter, as of 2026-11-09T04:02:43Z–04:04:22Z).

### Session cost puts the price gap at 20–50x for the same Claude Code session

OpenRouter's session-cost dataset gives the median dollars per session by harness and turn range, which is the closest thing to a measured cost per run. For **Claude Code sessions of 10–49 turns**, the medians are GPT-6 Luna $0.024, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash $0.027, MiMo-V2.6-Flash $0.027, GLM-5.3-Flash $0.038 and Claude Haiku 5.5 $0.043. Higher up are GPT-6.1 Sol $0.426, **Claude Sonnet 5.5 $0.534**, Kimi K3.1 Pro $0.563, Gemini 4 Argon $1.246, **Claude Opus 5.5 $1.331** and Claude Fable 5.1 $3.681. At **50+ turns** the medians are DeepSeek V4.1 Flash $0.168, Claude Haiku 5.5 $0.733, GPT-6.1 Sol $2.303, Sonnet 5.5 $3.992 and Opus 5.5 $8.489. At 1 turn they range from $0.001 on DeepSeek V4 Flash to $0.058 on Sonnet 5.5 and $0.113 on Opus 5.5 (SC, as of 2026-11-02T07:25:14.332Z). The same model costs about the same in different harnesses. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash runs $0.027–0.037 per 10–49-turn session across Claude Code, Codex, Hermes and Kilo Code (SC, same as_of). These medians are the cost priors used in the catalog. They come with no spread and no success rate, so a cheap session that has to be redone is not reflected in them.

## Seven hundred models shrink to a short list once fit, policy and uptime apply

### The catalog is large, and media lives on separate routes

OpenRouter lists **705 models from 88 authors**, but the default `/models` call returns only the 461 that output text. The engine must pass `output_modalities=all` to see the 66 image, 33 video, 38 text-to-speech, 30 speech-to-text, 45 embedding and 12 rerank models ([OpenRouter API reference](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api/api-reference/models/list-all-models-and-their-properties.md); computed from [/models?output_modalities=all](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models?output_modalities=all), fetched 2026-11-09). Each media kind has its own route (`/images`, `/videos`, `/audio/speech`, `/audio/transcriptions`, `/embeddings`, `/rerank`). Among chat models, 314 accept images, 189 files or PDFs, **97 video and 46 audio**. **204 have a context window of at least 1M tokens**, and only the two `x-ai/grok-4.20` variants (and OpenRouter's routers) reach 2M. 351 support both tools and structured outputs (same source). Video input is concentrated: Google and Qwen account for half of the video-capable chat models. Claude, by contrast, takes text, image and file input and returns text only ([Anthropic models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview); [Claude vision FAQ](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). None of the 34 Anthropic ids on OpenRouter is an embedding, speech or media model (computed from [/models?output_modalities=all](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models?output_modalities=all)).

### Prices span four orders of magnitude, and the cheapest capable model is unusually strong

Across 435 paid text models, the **median price is $0.50 per 1M input tokens and $2.00 per 1M output**, and half of all output prices fall between $0.60 and $6.00. **GLM-5.3-Flash costs $0.0335/$0.083** and has 1M context, image and video input, tools and structured outputs, 36 providers and ZDR endpoints. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (2026-10-17 snapshot) lists $0.006/$0.50 with 40 providers. GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 both list $0.10/$0.50 (computed from [/models?output_modalities=all](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models?output_modalities=all), fetched 2026-11-09). Claude models on OpenRouter cost exactly what Anthropic charges directly: Haiku 5.5 $0.10/$0.50, Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10, Opus 5.5 $4/$20 and Fable 5.1 $10/$50 ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [models.dev](https://models.dev/api.json)). So the PME can score Claude from the same price table as every other model. Four pricing details change rankings in practice. First, **Haiku 5.5's price rises fivefold, to $0.50/$2.50, once a prompt passes 100k tokens**, and 83 models carry similar long-context overrides. Second, the model-level price is not always the cheapest endpoint: Gemini 3.8 Flash lists $0.825/$4.125, but its flex tier costs $0.375/$1.875 ([endpoints: gemini-3.8-flash](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/google/gemini-3.8-flash/endpoints)). Third, `:batch` variants cost exactly half, but none of their endpoints is zero-retention. Fourth, reasoning tokens bill as output even when hidden ([OpenRouter reasoning tokens](https://openrouter.ai/docs/use-cases/reasoning-tokens)). OpenRouter also takes 5.5% when credits are bought, not per request, so effective cash cost is list × 1.055 ([OpenRouter pricing](https://openrouter.ai/pricing)).

### Uptime is high at the top and fragile in the tail

For the top 60 models by weekly usage, the best endpoint showed **100% one-day uptime for 57 of the 59 with data**. Individual endpoints vary much more: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash on one host and Step 5 Preview on another sat at 0%. **419 of 705 models have a single provider**, so one endpoint's outage takes the whole model down. Media models are noisier still: Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash showed 82.1%, Seedance 2.0 88.2% and Lyria 3 Pro Preview 54.1% (computed from per-model [endpoints responses](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/anthropic/claude-sonnet-4.5/endpoints), fetched 2026-11-09). OpenRouter defines uptime as the share of minutes in which at least 80% of attempts succeeded, counting only minutes with 10 or more attempts, so a null value means "too little traffic", not "down" ([endpoints reference](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api/api-reference/endpoints/list-all-endpoints-for-a-model.md)). The reliability term should therefore use the endpoint OpenRouter is likely to route to, and single-provider models should carry a penalty or a named fallback.

### "No training" is enforceable; "zero retention" removes Gemini and Fable

Of 2,444 endpoints, **1,055 are zero-data-retention (ZDR)**, 55 are Safety Retention (kept only for abuse detection, never trained on), and 1,334 are not on the ZDR list. The unlisted group includes every first-party Anthropic and OpenAI endpoint, even though both companies' API terms exclude training ([OpenRouter ZDR docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr.md); computed from [/endpoints/zdr](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/endpoints/zdr)). Absence from the list therefore does not mean a provider trains on prompts. p0ssess's default ("only providers that do not train on prompts") is best enforced by sending `provider.data_collection: "deny"` on every delegated call, with `zdr: true` as a stricter optional mode ([provider selection](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection)). The strict mode has a real cost in coverage. **No Gemini model in the top 60 has a ZDR endpoint**, only Safety Retention on Vertex. Claude Fable 5.x has none either. Claude Haiku, Sonnet and Opus reach ZDR through Bedrock and Google endpoints (same sources). Gemini 3.8 Flash is the default pick for most audio and video rows in the catalog, so a strict-ZDR user falls back to MiMo-V2.6-Flash, GLM-5.3-Flash and the ZDR speech models. For most open-weight models ZDR costs nothing extra. The cheapest ZDR endpoint was the cheapest endpoint overall for the median model, the 75th percentile paid 1.45x, and the 90th paid 2.0x, mostly where a discounted non-ZDR tier exists (computed from [/endpoints/zdr](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/endpoints/zdr) and per-model endpoints). None of the 23 `:free` variants is ZDR, and 13 had no live endpoint, which makes them poor defaults.

### The agent gap is the most consequential number in the evidence

Most families show the cheap models close to Claude. On text, coding chat, GPQA and tool use, GLM-5.3-Flash and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash land within one confidence interval or a few points of Sonnet 5.5 at one-twentieth of the price or less. The two boards that measure long, open-ended agent work tell a different story. On LMArena's Agent Arena, the cheap models score about zero against Opus 5.5's 0.201. On FrontierMath Tier 4, the hardest set, GLM-5.3-Flash falls to 0.171 against Opus 5.5's 0.95. This matters because agent workflow execution is OpenRouter's largest token pool (21.8%, CT, as of 2026-11-08). The work that looks most attractive to offload by volume is exactly where the clean evidence says quality falls off. A plain ratio of quality to cost hides this: a 50x price gap swamps a quality gap of 0.2 versus 0.02. The scoring term that captures it is **expected cost per accepted result**, which includes the cost of Claude redoing failed work (the redo rate *r* in the next section). GPT-6.1 Sol is the notable middle option. It scores 0.125 on Agent Arena at a $0.98 median task cost, against Sonnet's 0.154 at $3.72, which is why the catalog recommends it for long and background agent work. Uncertainty is uneven, too. Agent Arena intervals for Haiku 5.5 and MiMo-V2.6-Flash cross zero, and LMArena agent data starts only in 2026-06 ([LMArena dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmarena-ai/leaderboard-dataset)).

### Coverage holes and disagreements the engine must handle

Effort changes scores more than the error bars. GPQA for Sonnet 5.5 is 0.956 at Epoch's `_max` and 0.928 in OpenRouter's run, and Haiku 5.5 is 0.897 against 0.867 ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/data/benchmark_data.zip); OE, as of 2026-11-09T00:00:58Z). Evidence should therefore be keyed by `(model, effort)`. Nine of the 40 most-used OpenRouter entries (one is the aggregated "other" row) have no clean evidence in any allowed source, among them the jev classifiers, Muse Spark 1.3 Contributor, Stealth Crouton Alpha and Solar Mini4. Step 5 Preview, with 34.1T tokens over 30 days, appears only on LMArena (join of the four sources against RD, as of 2026-11-09T04:00:01.004Z). Claude is among the best-covered families: Opus 5.5 appears in 4 Epoch-run benchmarks, 12 LMArena subsets and 2 OpenRouter evals. The name join also misses things. LMArena's top image model, GPT-Image-2.5 Sunburst, has the same name as an OpenRouter listing but was left unjoined, so the registry needs a human-checked alias table. Aider's polyglot file (Apache-2.0) is useful only as method. Its newest entry is from 2025-10-03, it covers no current model, but its `percent_cases_well_formed` field is the right shape for an edit-format reliability metric in p0ssess's own eval set ([Aider polyglot leaderboard](https://github.com/Aider-AI/aider/blob/main/aider/website/_data/polyglot_leaderboard.yml)). For media understanding, where p0ssess will route most automatically, there is no clean benchmark at all. Logging outcomes for those delegations from day one is the only way to fill the gap.

## Delegation costs Claude $0.04–0.12 before the worker spends a cent

### What a unit of work costs on Claude and elsewhere

Claude's API cost is exactly computable: tokens by category × list price. Claude Code computes the same figure locally ([Claude Code costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs); [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)). Two published rates matter most for sizing: cache reads cost 5% of input on Opus and Sonnet 5.5, and the Claude 4.7+ tokenizer produces about 30% more tokens for the same text. Task size should be stored as formulas built from published conversion rates, not as constants. Text runs about 4 bytes per token. A PDF page costs 1,500–3,000 tokens on Claude and is also processed as an image ([PDF support](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/pdf-support)). Gemini counts audio at 32 tokens per second and video at about 100–300 tokens per second; Google's page is internally inconsistent on the default video rate ([Google AI Studio tokens](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/tokens)). From these rates come representative comparisons. Thirty minutes of audio costs about $0.048 on Gemini 3.8 Flash, or $0.006–0.15 on dedicated speech-to-text models *if* their price field is per second (OpenRouter does not document the unit). Ten minutes of video costs **$0.05–0.15 on Gemini 3.8 Flash**, against roughly $1.87 for a frame-by-frame workaround on Sonnet 5.5 (that figure assumes one frame per second). A 300-page PDF costs **$1.8–2.7 on Sonnet** and $0.46–0.68 on Haiku 5.5 in its over-100k tier, against about $0.60 for Mistral OCR plus $0.02–0.05 for a DeepSeek summary ([OpenRouter PDFs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/overview/multimodal/pdfs)). A 1 MB log costs about $0.65 for Sonnet's first read and about $0.013 on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. For code review, log triage and bulk renames, cost is driven less by input size than by Claude's per-round context re-reads. Claude Code sends the whole conversation with every tool round ([Claude Code costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)), so these tasks should be estimated as rounds × cached context × cache rate + output.

### Subscription limits have exactly one documented dollar anchor

For subscribers, the documentation gives only relative limit sizes. Max is "5x Pro usage every 5 hours and every week" and Ultra is "10x Pro usage every week" with no 5-hour limit for verified accounts. No token, message or dollar size is published ([claude.com/pricing](https://claude.com/pricing); [Help Center: Claude Code with Pro or Max](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11145838-using-claude-code-with-your-pro-or-max-plan)). Limits are shared across claude.ai and Claude Code. Fable has its own weekly bucket on Max and Ultra. Long sessions keep drawing usage "for the whole conversation" at the cached rate ([Claude Code costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)). **The only documented dollar figure is that usage past a limit, through usage credits, is "billed at standard API rates"** ([Help Center: usage credits](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12429409-extra-usage-for-paid-claude-plans)). So a subscriber's marginal cash cost of Claude is $0 until a window runs out, list price after that if credits are on, and "blocked until reset" if they are off. The machine-readable signal is the status-line `rate_limits.five_hour` and `seven_day` objects, each with `used_percentage` and `resets_at`. They appear only for Pro, Max and Ultra, only after the first response, and either window can be missing ([status line docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/statusline)). How tokens map onto `used_percentage` is undocumented: the weights of input, output, cache and thinking tokens, and whether claude.ai chat counts the same, are all unknown.

The PME therefore has to choose a pressure function rather than look one up. Let *L* be the list-price dollars of the Claude tokens a route would use. For each window, let *û* be projected end-of-window use, u / max(elapsed fraction, e_min), and take the maximum across windows. Four options follow:

| Option | Claude cost C | Anchored where documented? | Strength | Weakness | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Always list | C = L | Yes, matches usage-credit price | Simple; matches Claude Code's own figure | Overstates cost far from limits and biases toward delegating, which measurably *raises* Claude use on small tasks | API-key plans; a "conservative" subscription preset |
| B. Zero until scarce | C = L · clamp((û − θ)/(1 − θ), 0, 1); with credits off, × (1 + β · max(û − 1, 0)) | Yes, C = L at projected exhaustion | Tracks true marginal cost | Claude is free below θ, then a cliff; θ and β are pure policy; projections are noisy early in a window | Building block, not a default |
| C. Amortized plan price | C = L · plan_price / K̂, with K̂ learned from paired readings | No, plan capacity K is undocumented | Shows what the plan is worth | Average is not marginal; K̂ is biased by usage on other devices and claude.ai; drifts whenever Anthropic changes limits | Display on `/p0ssess-me` only |
| D. Smooth curve with a floor | C = L · (ε + (1 − ε) · û^k), clamped to L when credits are on | Yes at û = 1, same as B | No cliff; one dial maps cleanly to "save Claude" | ε and k are invented shape parameters | Subscriptions |

**Recommendation:** use A on API-key plans. On subscriptions, use D with B's anchor, so that Claude's cost reaches exactly list price at projected exhaustion and the weekly window governs "save Claude" mode, because a weekly limit costs days rather than hours. Show C only as information. When there is no reading, D falls back to its floor ε. None of ε, k, β, θ or e_min is a sourced fact, so this research gives no default values for them. p0ssess should ship them as plainly labeled user presets, record the preset with every routing decision, and calibrate them from acceptance and redo history. The floor is what separates D from B. It keeps a large Claude job from looking free early in a window, a real cost because the limit is shared with claude.ai and other devices. And it prevents the abrupt flip in routing that B produces when pressure crosses θ.

### Small tasks cost more when delegated, so thresholds must sit above ten turns

Two independent measurements agree on the overhead. The public oc-connect study ran three small coding tasks four ways, with Sonnet 5.5 as lead and a *free* DeepSeek worker. Claude alone used $0.471 of Claude list-price usage in 128 s. A plain hand-off without research used $0.519 (+10%). Hand-off with research used $0.732 (+55%), and the plugin path $0.861 (+83%), while taking 7–20x longer. Its conclusion: "At this task size delegating costs MORE Claude tokens than doing it, even with a free worker" ([oc-connect measured.md](https://github.com/jonaandresen/oc-connect/blob/main/docs/measured.md)). An unpublished local reproduction on 2026-11-08 (Claude Code 2.1.319, Sonnet 5.5 lead, Haiku 5.5 relay, OpenCode with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash) isolated the parts. The Haiku relay cost $0.0002–0.0011 per call, which is noise. **The lead model's hand-off turns cost $0.04–0.06, and $0.06–0.12 when it re-checked the work with its own tools**, while the worker spent $0.001–0.005 per small task. Both sources hit two traps that bear on p0ssess's design. A relay told to pass output through unchanged summarized it anyway, until the prompt required an exact `<answer>` block (8 of 8 exact afterward). And Claude Code's own cost display mis-prices gateway-routed tokens: it showed a phantom $0.115 in the reproduction, and $0.375 for a call billed $0.008 in OpenRouter's account ([OpenRouter blog](https://openrouter.ai/blog/tutorials/claude-code-usage-limit/)). The engine has to keep its own ledger.

This overhead sets a hard rule. Write the delegated route's cost as OpenRouter cash + C(L_overhead + L_review) + r · C(L_direct), where *r* is the learned redo rate, and the Claude-direct route as C(L_direct). If C scales L by a pressure factor *p*, delegation is cheaper only when **cash < p · [(1 − r) · L_direct − L_overhead − L_review]**. If (1 − r) · L_direct is smaller than the overhead plus review, delegation never saves Claude usage, at any pressure. Pressure only decides how far above that line a task must be before OpenRouter cash is worth spending. On a Sonnet lead, the overhead is $0.04–0.12. Session-cost medians put a 2–9-turn Claude Code session on Sonnet at $0.134 and a 10–49-turn one at $0.534 (SC, as of 2026-11-02T07:25:14.332Z). oc-connect's $0.15–0.17 tasks still lost. So for coding work that Claude could do itself, **the default threshold should be roughly "predicted at least ten turns of lead-model work" (about $0.2–0.5 of Sonnet list-price usage)**, raised by any learned redo rate. To illustrate with r = 0, a 10–49-turn task delegated to DeepSeek for $0.027 beats Sonnet once p exceeds about 0.065, using the high-end $0.12 overhead. Every point of redo rate raises that bar. Opus-lead, large-task and repeated-run measurements do not exist yet, which oc-connect states openly, so the threshold is a starting prior. The rule does not apply to capability routes such as video, audio and image generation, where there is no Claude-direct option and the overhead is simply the price of having the capability. Direct-path one-shots with pass-through review sit near the low end of the overhead.

### How budgets should combine with these costs

The measurements imply four budget rules. First, keep two currencies. OpenRouter cash (× 1.055) is what the monthly cap and daily soft limit count. Pressure-weighted Claude usage enters the routing score but not the cash cap, as DESIGN.md defines the cap. Second, the $0.02 per-task auto-approve should gate *predicted OpenRouter cash* at a conservative estimate, either the p90 of past runs or the `max_tokens` worst case including reasoning. It should not gate Claude-side overhead. Under that rule, almost every coding and text delegation passes, while most media jobs ask: an image at about $0.04 ([OpenRouter image generation](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/overview/multimodal/image-generation)), ten minutes of video at $0.05–0.15, 300-page OCR at $0.60, an 8-second generated clip at $0.19 or more. Third, a learned per-category auto-approve ("approve image generation up to $X from now on") is the natural fix for that media friction. Fourth, a paced daily soft limit, (cap − spent) / days left, should lower auto-approve to zero when crossed rather than block. Pinning a provider order disables OpenRouter's sticky cache routing ([prompt caching](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/best-practices/prompt-caching)), so multi-turn agent sessions should avoid manual provider orders.

### Stakes-4 jobs are routing decisions, and redaction must stay local

Three stakes-4 jobs have cheap candidates that pass the fit gate but fail the stakes gate. **Security-critical code** (auth, sessions, crypto, permission checks, payments) is one. In a CCS 2023 user study, participants with an AI assistant "wrote significantly less secure code than those without access" and "were more likely to believe they wrote secure code" ([Perry et al., arXiv:2211.03622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03622)). Veracode's vendor report finds insecure choices in about 45% of 80 CWE-mapped tasks across 100+ models, with no independent replication ([Veracode, 2025-07-30](https://www.veracode.com/press-release/ai-generated-code-poses-major-security-risks-in-nearly-half-of-all-development-tasks-veracode-research-reveals/)). Checking costs about as much as writing here, so "cheap draft, Claude verifies" does not apply. **Production database migrations** are another: Prisma 6.15.0 added a consent variable so that "an AI coding tool" cannot run `prisma migrate reset` without permission ([Prisma blog](https://www.prisma.io/blog/orm-6-15-0-ai-safety-guardrails-for-destructive-commands-and-more)), after reports of agents running destructive migration commands ([claudeissues #34729](https://claudeissues.com/issue/34729-model-incident-report-database-data-loss-due-to-prisma-migration-reset), [#26913](https://claudeissues.com/issue/26913-agent-executed-destructive-database-command-alembic-downgrade-base-without-user); [AI Incident Database 1152](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/1152/)). **Infrastructure and firewall changes** are the third: "The reviewed plan remains the safety boundary" ([Scalr](https://scalr.com/learning-center/infrastructure-as-code-with-ai-coding-agents)), and the tfmcp Terraform MCP server keeps apply and destroy disabled by default ([tfmcp](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/nwiizo/tfmcp@main/README.md)). Recognizing these three should also lower the engine's offer rate for adjacent jobs: SQL, devops config, shell commands and feature work.

PII redaction is the inverse case. Sending data to an outside model in order to redact it defeats the purpose, so the route is a local tool such as Microsoft Presidio, with Claude reviewing a sample and every high-risk field. Presidio itself warns "there is no guarantee that Presidio will find all sensitive information" ([Presidio](https://github.com/microsoft/presidio)).

### A cheap draft plus a check pays when the check is mechanical

The four strongest new delegation patterns reject bad output with a local check that costs nothing in model spend:

- **Test-gated bulk migration.** Airbnb migrated about 3.5K React test files from Enzyme to React Testing Library in six weeks against a 1.5-engineering-year estimate, gating each file on Jest, lint and `tsc` and re-prompting with the errors; 97% converted after four days, and "retrying conversion multiple times" beat prompt engineering ([InfoQ, 2025-03-28](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/03/airbnb-llm-test-migration/); Airbnb's own post returned HTTP 403). Google reports that 80% of the code changes in one migration's landed CLs were fully AI-authored and only code that passes unit tests is produced ([arXiv:2501.06972](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.06972v1), self-reported). On DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 one 100k-token attempt costs about $0.002, against about $0.23 on Sonnet before caching.
- **Generate and filter tests.** Meta's TestGen-LLM keeps only tests that build, pass reliably and raise coverage: 75% built, 57% passed reliably, 25% raised coverage, and engineers accepted 73% of its recommendations ([arXiv:2402.09171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09171)). Its mutation-guided ACH reached 73% acceptance ([arXiv:2501.12862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12862)).
- **Fuzz harnesses.** OSS-Fuzz-gen judges each generated target on compiling, running and coverage, and reports 30 new bugs ([google/oss-fuzz-gen](https://github.com/google/oss-fuzz-gen)). Crash triage stays with Claude, because those findings are security work.
- **Localization with placeholder parity.** One practitioner's pipeline shipped a literal `VAR_1` "to production, in seven languages, for weeks"; a placeholder-diff check found 47 broken strings on its first run ([DEV](https://dev.to/pakvothe/our-ai-translation-pipeline-shipped-var1-to-production-in-7-languages-5fag), single source). Tools such as [lingo.dev](https://github.com/lingodotdev/lingo.dev) and [ai-i18n](https://github.com/i18n-actions/ai-i18n) send only changed strings.

Where verification needs Claude to look at each item (alt text, the judgment parts of a postmortem), the saving shrinks to whatever sampling allows. Claude viewing an image costs about as much as generating its alt text.

### Small text jobs sit below the overhead line inside a session

Commit messages and PR bodies cost Sonnet about $0.009 for a 4k-token diff, against the measured $0.04–0.12 hand-off overhead, so the engine should route them only where no Claude session is involved: a `prepare-commit-msg` hook such as [OpenCommit](https://github.com/di-sukharev/opencommit), CI or batch jobs. Release notes and postmortems are larger and clear the line. Release notes start with a deterministic grouping step (GitHub's label-driven generator needs no model), then a cheap model writes prose that a script checks against real PR numbers ([SmartNote, arXiv:2505.17977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17977)). Postmortems start with a local grep; incident.io's own product calls its draft "a starting point, not a finished document" ([incident.io](https://docs.incident.io/post-incident/postmortem-ai)).

### Model picks for the new jobs follow proxies

No clean benchmark exists for translation accuracy, extraction accuracy, deduplication, reranking, safety classification, test generation or alt text, so picks rest on the closest LMArena categories (rev f566816, as of 2026-11-06, CC BY 4.0). On non-English text, Opus 5.5 rates 1493.0, Gemini 3.8 Flash 1488.2, GPT-6.1 Sol 1469.5, GLM-5.3-Flash 1465.3 and Haiku 5.5 1407.0. On OCR, Gemini 3.8 Flash rates 1315.5 and Opus 5.5 1311.8. Gemini 3.8 Flash is therefore the best cheap translator and document reader, but it has no ZDR endpoint, so strict-ZDR users fall to GLM-5.3-Flash. Opus 5.5 is the right translator for stakes 3–4 strings. Two capability routes have gaps of their own. OpenRouter's 12 rerank models show 0/0 prices in the public model and endpoint APIs, so the rerank route should stay off until a price is exposed or the local ledger records a billed amount ([OpenRouter RAG cookbook](https://openrouter.ai/docs/cookbook/evaluate-and-optimize/rag.md)). For safety screening of untrusted text, gpt-oss-safeguard-20b classifies against a written policy, including a prompt-injection policy in Groq's docs, but it is labeled Preview and has one provider ([Groq](https://console.groq.com/docs/model/openai/gpt-oss-safeguard-20b)); it is an additive filter, never the only defense ([Claude Code security](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/security)).

## The catalog

**How to read it.** Recommendations are starting priors. Each starts from the fit gate (modalities, context, tools), weighs clean quality evidence where it exists and usage share where it doesn't, prices in redo risk, and then applies the stakes gate, so Claude is recommended where the evidence or the stakes favor it. Costs are estimates for the recommended model at list price before OpenRouter's 5.5% credit fee. Token jobs use list prices from the [OpenRouter model API](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models?output_modalities=all) (fetched 2026-11-09) and five size profiles, which are assumptions, not measurements: **T** = 1k in / 50 out per item; **S** = 5k / 1k; **M** = 30k / 3k; **R** = 30k / 6k (review with reasoning, billed as output); **L** = 200k / 4k. Agent rows use session-cost medians for the Claude Code harness by turn range. Media rows use the formulas above; speech-to-text and text-to-speech assume the per-second or per-character unit OpenRouter has not confirmed. Budget sensitivity and verification levels follow DESIGN.md's budget and review rules; the catalog has a field-by-field guide.

Several patterns should shape the engine's defaults. **The media and embedding rows (#59, #70–77, #80–87) have no Claude-direct option at all.** They are the cleanest routing wins, and also the rows with the least benchmark evidence and the most single-provider, non-ZDR, sub-99%-uptime models. **Five rows put a local tool first** (#22, #23, #78, #79, #97), and four more start with a local step before a model (#55, #88, #90, #94), so the candidate set needs a "no model" entry. **Ten rows keep the work on Claude**: three because of measured overhead (#1, #11, and #89, commit text inside a session), one because of quality (#17, where Claude leads the front-end board by a wide margin), three because stakes moved them (#58, #60, #61), and three stakes-4 jobs (#98–100). Every review and judgment row (#28–40) prefers a non-Anthropic family. The reason is the family self-preference research, which reports that judges favor their own family's outputs even with objective rubrics. That finding is contested, and no controlled study shows cross-family code review catching more real bugs. So these rows deserve the opt-in evaluation set's earliest attention.

## Conclusion

The market and the evidence point in different directions, and p0ssess should follow the evidence. OpenRouter's traffic says the world already hands agentic coding to flash models at a twentieth of Claude's price. On the only clean agentic benchmark those models score near zero, against 0.15–0.20 for Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5, and the delegation measurements say small hand-offs lose Claude usage outright. The engine's first-order job is therefore not to mirror the market. It should send capability gaps and bulk one-shot work out by default and treat agentic delegation as a priced risk. That only works if the score is reframed from quality × fit ÷ cost to expected cost per accepted result, with the learned redo rate inside it and stakes setting both the harm of a bad result and the ceiling a model must clear. That change makes the break-even rule fall out of the arithmetic instead of a tuned constant: if (1 − r) × Claude's direct cost does not exceed the hand-off and review overhead, no pressure level justifies delegating. It also explains where cheap models win at higher stakes: when a mechanical check makes `p_fail` small and checking far cheaper than doing.

Two gaps decide how good routing can get, and only p0ssess can close them. The families where routing is most automatic, audio, video and media generation, have no clean quality benchmark and the shakiest uptime. And the subscription limit has no published size, so every pressure curve rests on user presets until local readings calibrate it. The same is true of the `p_fail` ceiling for each stakes level. The opt-in evaluation set and the local delegation history are therefore core infrastructure, not add-ons. They should log outcomes, redo rates, stakes ratings and preset choices from the first delegation. The public evidence supports 104 distinct jobs once stakes are taken into account. Further entries should come from what p0ssess's own users actually hand off.
